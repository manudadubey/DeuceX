This one is not a primer

Different from the rest
Every other tool in here is built for the twenty minutes before you walk on. This one is not. The evidence behind it is about hours accumulated over weeks, and the trials needed at least ten of them before anything showed up.
So four minutes before a match will not do what the research describes. Use it as ordinary training on ordinary days, and if you do use it before a match, use it because it wakes you up, not because you think you are banking anything.

What Field does not do

Read this
This is the useful field of view task. It has the best transfer evidence of anything in this category: a review of forty four studies from seventeen randomised trials found effects three to five times larger than other cognitive training, transfer to everyday function, fewer crashes among older drivers, and gains that held for ten years.
Read that carefully though. It was older adults, not young athletes. The transfer was to driving and daily life, not to sport. And about half of those trials were run by people with a financial interest in the training being sold.
The original puts targets out to thirty degrees from centre. A phone at arm's length is only about twenty three degrees wide in total, so this reaches roughly a third of that. The outer ring, which is where a lot of the driving effect sat, cannot be tested on a phone at all.
Your screen cannot flash anything shorter than one refresh. If your reading lands on that floor it is the screen you are measuring, not you, and the summary will say so.
Nothing is sent anywhere, and no part of this tells you whether you are ready to play.
