When to use it

Before you play
One run, once. The Primer is about three and a half minutes and that is the point. Doing three runs back to back is how you arrive tired rather than switched on.
Finish twenty to thirty minutes before the first ball, and keep the last ten minutes before you walk on screen free.
Phone away before you walk on court. Players may not receive information through an electronic device during a match.

What Hold does not do

Read this
This is a known task. It is called multiple object tracking, and in three dimensions it is the paradigm behind the commercial trackers a lot of clubs already own.
Here is the honest part. People reliably get better at this task. The evidence that getting better at it makes you better at your sport is contested, and the reviews that go looking for it tend to find small studies without proper control groups. We are not going to tell you otherwise.
So it sits here as a primer and nothing else. The claim is that it switches your attention on in the minutes before you play, not that it changes how you play in six weeks. If your speed number climbs, the most likely explanation is that you got better at this, not at tennis.
The original is viewed through 3D glasses and your two eyes do real work. A phone has one flat screen, so depth here comes from size, overlap and shading only.
Nothing is sent anywhere, and no part of this tells you whether you are ready to play.
