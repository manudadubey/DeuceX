When to use it

Before you play
Six to ten minutes is the ceiling, not the target. One set is about two minutes. Two sets is plenty and a third is working against you.
Finish twenty to thirty minutes before the first ball. The last ten minutes before you walk on should have no screen in them at all.
Phone away before you walk on court. Players may not receive information through an electronic device during a match, and this is not worth a conversation with a supervisor.

What Margin does not do

Read this
It does not claim to make you win more. Cutting the picture off early and making players predict what happens next has a good research record for anticipation, including on court and not only on a screen. This particular drill, on a phone, has not been tested against anything.
That research is mostly about reading an opponent's body before they hit. Reading the flight of the ball after they hit is a near neighbour, not the same thing.
A screen has one eye, not two, so the depth you use here is not the depth you use on court. The whole court is also squeezed into a much smaller angle than it fills in front of you.
The camera sits behind and above where you actually stand. From your real eye position the baseline at your feet does not fit on a phone at all, so this view is a compromise and the drill is a judgment exercise, not a copy of what you see.
Your calls stay on your phone. There is no score sent anywhere, and nothing here tells you whether you are ready to play.
