Design system

Baseline

The baseline is the line a player plays from. It is also the honest number the product starts from every morning: how long the money lasts, what decision is due, who joined. Baseline is the visual and written system for everything DeuceX ships, from the player dashboard to the admin console, so that a number, a proposal and a warning look the same wherever they appear and always mean the same thing.

Where it comes from. Every token here was captured from the shadcn/ui preset b3REZz9P6 (style Vega, base Neutral, chart palette Lime, Geist, Lucide, radius 0.625rem, Base UI primitives) as rendered in the live preview, then measured against the player prototypes. Nothing is a guess.
What it covers. Foundations, components, the five product patterns that make DeuceX itself (the three answers, the decision card, the approval gate, locked surfaces, the shells), the writing rules, accessibility, and how to implement it in Tailwind and CSS variables.

Principles

1
The agent proposes, the player decides
Every proposal carries a one-sentence consequence next to its control. Nothing leaves the app on a tap the player has not read. A confirm step is reserved for actions the outside world cannot undo; everything else is undoable in one tap, so confirmations stay rare enough to be read.
2
One accent, and it means data
Lime is the chart palette and the accent. It is never used to mean good. Good, warning and danger have their own tokens so a green bar cannot be misread as a green light.
3
Numbers are the interface
Every figure sits in Geist Mono with tabular digits, right-aligned in tables, with its unit and its basis nearby. A number without its source is a claim, not a fact.
4
Show uncertainty, never hide it
Stale, pending, unverified and estimated are visible states with their own words and tokens. An agent that is unsure says so in the same place it would have said the answer.
5
Phone in a hotel corridor
Every route works at 390px with no horizontal scroll; primary decisions are two taps from the dashboard; pressables give pointer-down feedback; chrome is translucent and content scrolls underneath.
6
Restraint
Cards use one radius and one hairline ring. Status is carried by colour token and a dot, not by icons piled on badges. Motion is spring-like, short, and off when the system asks.

Colour

A neutral scale in OKLCH with zero chroma, one lime chart ramp, and three semantic status tokens. Light and dark are both first-class: the dark palette is its own set of steps, not an inversion. Toggle the theme in the header to see both.

Neutral scale

Lime chart ramp

Five steps, used in order for series. chart-2 is the accent for bars, rails and the selected state. Light mode shifts each step one darker so the ramp keeps contrast on white.

Status

Kept apart from lime on purpose. Each has a 10 to 14 percent background variant for badges and tile veils.

Rules. Lime means data or selection, never good. Amber means a threshold is near (runway under 10 weeks, 28°C, 70 percent humidity, a deadline inside 10 days). Red means a loss, a failure or a destructive action. Green means a completed, positive fact (paid, verified, connected). Muted foreground is for secondary text and never for a number the player must read.
Surfaces. --background for the page, --card for cards, --surface (#f8f8f8 / #161616) for insets inside a card such as a draft or a confirm block, --field (3 percent / 4.5 percent) for inputs and outline buttons, --secondary at 50 percent for list items. Sidebar has its own tokens.

Uppercase labels always carry letter-spacing. No weight above 600 anywhere; 600 is for avatar initials only.

Motion

Motion in Baseline follows one idea: the interface behaves like a physical thing. It responds on pointer-down, moves continuously, carries the user's momentum, can be grabbed mid-flight, and leaves the way it came. The prototypes approximate this with CSS transitions; the build uses springs for anything a finger can hold.

Interruptible always. A closing sheet the player grabs again follows the finger; it does not finish closing first. Every spring starts from the on-screen value, carries the current velocity through a re-target, and decomposes X and Y. Input is never locked during a transition.
Reduced motion is gentler feedback, not none. With prefers-reduced-motion, transforms stop animating but opacity, colour and shadow still fade over 150ms, sheets cross-fade instead of sliding, and the press state still changes. prefers-reduced-transparency makes chrome and sheets opaque. prefers-contrast: more replaces hairline rings with a foreground ring. A theme change eases over 200ms rather than jumping.

Icons and flags

Icons

Lucide, 16px, stroke 2, round caps and joins, inline SVG with currentColor. In the tab bar 22px at stroke 1.75. Icons never carry meaning alone; they sit beside a word.

Flags

An inline SVG sprite, 15×10px with 2px radius and a half-pixel inset ring; 13×9 inside badges. Never emoji (no emoji font in headless and some Windows builds; wide glyphs broke wrapping). Flags appear on players, tournaments, cities and currencies; never on languages, never inside a <select>.

Cards and tiles

The card is the unit of the page. Radius 14, no border, a hairline ring, 24px vertical padding with each child padded 24px horizontally, 24px gap between children; on mobile 20 and 16. Toasts are 13px, popover surface, bottom centre, 2.6 seconds, never stacked. They confirm what just happened in the same words the consequence sentence used.

Tooltip

Any element with data-tip gets a 12px foreground-on-background tooltip after 350ms, above by default, below when there is no room, with an arrow. Help marks are 14px circles with a question mark. Tooltips explain a term or a threshold; they never hide the consequence sentence.

Sheets and menus

Notification rail: 400px from the right, translucent, day groups with For you and FYI filters and Mark all read. Quick actions: 320px from the bottom right on desktop, bottom sheet on mobile. User menu: opens upward from the sidebar footer, 220px minimum, sign out in red. All trap focus, close on Escape and scrim tap, and respect reduced transparency.

Charts

Hand-drawn SVG through a small el() helper, redrawn on resize, gradient ids suffixed by host id. Text 11px in the sans, mono for values. Money axes go through axisK() so a currency change redraws every axis.

Series and marks
Lime steps in order; the current point filled; projections dashed; defence weeks in amber; today as a hairline.
Rules. One y-axis per chart, always. Grid at 6 or 7 percent. Bars 4px radius, 2px gap. Lines 2px. A single series has no legend; two or more have one, with direct labels on up to four. Colour follows the entity, never its rank. Amber and red in a chart mean the same as in a badge. Every chart has an aria-label that states its claim, and the three morning answers are also written as sentences under their tiles.

Enter motion. Marks draw in when a chart mounts or its data changes, the way shadcn charts do: lines draw along their length over 600ms, bars grow from the baseline over 400ms with a 25ms stagger, areas and point markers fade in after the line; axes, grid and labels never move. A resize redraw does not animate. Meters, progress bars and sparklines grow from their origin on route entry. Everything uses cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1); under reduced motion each becomes a 200ms fade. A value in a tile never counts up.

Pattern · the three answers

The dashboard opens with the ranking hero and then exactly three pulse tiles, in this order: how long the money lasts, what decision is due, who joined or left. Each tile is a button that goes to its agent. The order is the product's premise; research may move it, design must not.

Pattern · the decision card

One card per week, the Tournament Agent's top pick, with the facts as chips, cost to go, outcomes by round, the net outcome rail, the runway after a loss and after a good week, and two controls. Pattern · the approval gate

Anything that leaves the app (an entry, a payment, an email, a post, a message) is a two-step, or a one-tap with the consequence sentence printed beside the control in the same glance. The words are part of the component.

Writing the sentence. Name the amount or the count, the recipient, the timing, and whether it can be undone. Present tense, active voice, under 25 words. Never "Are you sure?". The toast that follows uses the same nouns.

Pattern · locked surfaces

A gated feature shows its real layout with sample data, dimmed under a veil, with one line naming what the plan adds and one action. Never a blank page, never two actions, never a page that looks broken.

Pattern · shells and routes

App shell. 256px sidebar (48px collapsed, Cmd/Ctrl+B), sticky translucent topbar with the page title, date chip, share, tour, bell and theme, content at 1400px max (1600 collapsed), one <main data-route> per route with hash routing. Under 1180px the sidebar hides and a six-tab translucent bar appears with a floating capture button.
Bare shell. Onboarding, sign-in and the coach view drop the sidebar and topbar: centred column, logo, steps. The coach view is read-only and shows no money anywhere.
Admin shell. The same shell with an environment badge, a global player search (⌘K) and a role preview. Areas outside the role are removed, not disabled. Every account action is a confirm block; some require a reason.
Storage. Each prototype namespaces its keys (pc.*, pc.ln.*, pc.sb.*, pc.admin.*) so several can be open in one browser.

Voice and copy

Tone. A knowledgeable friend who has done the arithmetic. Short sentences, no exclamation marks, no marketing adjectives. Australian English (colour, organise, kilometre). Never an em dash: commas, colons, parentheses, en dashes or a new sentence.
Agents speak in the first person only when the player would. The product says "The Tournament Agent's top pick"; drafts say "I lost 6-4". Agents never diagnose, never use clinical language, never name a model provider in the interface; providers are listed under Data & safety.
Numbers. Always with a unit and a basis: "8.3 weeks · A$9,450 reserves · A$1,140/wk net burn". Currency symbol in the home currency, original currency underneath where it differs. Tension as "24/23 kg" or "53/51 lb" and the prose rewrites with the unit.
Uncertainty has words. "not refreshed", "unconfirmed", "illustrative", "estimate", "pending", "unverified". Each has a token: muted text, an amber badge, or a dashed line. Never hide a stale figure by leaving it unchanged.

Accessibility

Contrast. Foreground on background clears 12:1 in both themes; muted foreground clears 4.5:1; lime is never the only carrier of meaning and never used for text on a light ground below 16px.
Focus and targets. Visible focus ring at 50 percent of --ring on every control; 44px minimum targets on mobile for buttons, switches, checkboxes, tabs and toggle groups; sidebar collapse and ⌘K on the keyboard; sheets trap and return focus.
Semantics. Switches are role="switch" with aria-checked; tabs and toggle groups use aria-selected and aria-pressed; charts carry role="img" and a claim; the three answers exist as sentences as well as tiles.
Preferences. Reduced motion, reduced transparency and increased contrast are honoured globally (see Motion). Theme follows the system with a manual override that wins in both directions and eases between the two. In the build, sizes are set in rem so the player's text-size setting scales the layout with the type; the prototypes use px and are the one place this rule is not yet met.

Component checklist

        
Before a component ships: it renders in both themes from tokens only; every grid child has an explicit column; text can wrap or scroll and never clips; it has a focus state and a pressed state; any figure is mono and tabular; any action that leaves the app has its consequence sentence; it has been seen at 390px.
