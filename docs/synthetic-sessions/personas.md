# Eight synthetic personas

All eight are invented. No name, ranking or circumstance below belongs to a real player. The moderator in every session is Manu Dubey, the founder, who is a stranger to all but two of them (P3 and P6 were introduced by a coach who knows Manu; the rest came through a federation list, a stringer or a WhatsApp group).

## P1 · Tomasz Krol · Stage 1 · junior turning pro
18, Polish, right-handed. ITF world tennis number 1,380 in the men's ranking, no ATP points, UTR 12.9 (which he checks more often than the ITF number because college coaches in the US email him about it). Turned down two US college offers in June to try the pro route for a year. Parents (a dentist and a schoolteacher in Poznań) fund everything from a shared family account and his mother books the flights. No travelling coach; his club coach sends him a WhatsApp voice note before matches. Plays M15 and M25 events in Poland, Czechia, Serbia and Turkey. Has never kept a receipt; his mother asks for them and he photographs some. Loses often in qualifying and does not talk about it with anyone except, occasionally, a hitting partner two years older. Fluent English, thinks in Polish. Sceptical that anything on a phone can help his tennis; open to anything that stops his mother asking about money. Has a small Instagram following (900) and would be mortified to ask any of them for money. Uses TikTok for tennis clips. Shown the Arya prototype.

## P2 · Priya Nair · Stage 1 · WTA, part-time worker
24, Australian, from Melbourne, left-handed. WTA #890, ITF women's #410, UTR 11.8. Plays the Australian and Asian ITF W15 and W35 swings and coaches juniors at a club three days a week between trips, which pays for roughly half her season. No coach on the road; a club coach reviews video when she is home. Keeps a Google Sheet of every expense because Tennis Australia's small travel grant requires acquittals. Knows her runway to the week because she has to. Has told nobody about the two months she nearly quit last year except her sister. Has a supportive club community who have offered to "chip in" and she has always said no, out of pride. Very aware that everything tennis-tech is built for men first; will notice immediately that the prototype shows an ATP player and that WTA is not mentioned anywhere. Practical, direct, allergic to marketing language. Shown the Arya prototype.

## P3 · Mateus Ferreira · Stage 2 · spreadsheet keeper
23, Brazilian, based in Barcelona for the European clay season, right-handed. ATP #640, ITF #300. Shares a coach with two other Brazilian players (they split his fee three ways and he travels with whichever of them has the best week coming). Keeps a detailed spreadsheet of costs per tournament and a second tab that guesses prize money; he is proud of it and slightly defensive about it. Money comes from prize money, a small Brazilian state sports grant paid quarterly and late, and a family friend who sends R$2,000 some months. He decided his next three tournaments by checking last year's cut-offs on the ATP site and asking the coach which weeks suit the other two players. Lost a match in Genoa last week he should have won and told his coach, then sat in his room. Writes nothing down about matches. Would like a patron page but worries about how it looks in Brazil ("like begging"). Was introduced by the shared coach, who knows Manu. Shown the Arya prototype.

## P4 · Lena Hartmann · Stage 2 · WTA, sponsor and parents
26, German, from Stuttgart, right-handed. WTA #520. A regional car dealership pays her €800 a month for a logo on her sleeve and two appearances a year; her parents cover the rest and her father keeps a folder of every invoice. Runs a decent Instagram (6,400 followers) with match-day stories and has been asked by followers how they can support her; she has never had an answer. Plays W35 to W75 events across Europe and one Asian swing. Coach is a former player who comes to about a third of her events. After bad losses she writes long notes in her phone's Notes app in German and has never shown them to anyone. Cares about how a patron page would look to the dealership. Curious, articulate, will engage with pricing seriously because it would be her own money now that she has turned 26 and wants to stop asking her parents. Shown the Arya prototype.

## P5 · Ryan O'Connell · Stage 2 · the sceptic
27, American, played four years of NCAA Division I before turning pro, right-handed. ATP #710, UTR 13.6 (he still quotes UTR first, out of college habit). Lives out of a suitcase between US Challengers, Mexican and Canadian ITF events, and a European block in summer. No coach; a former college teammate travels with him some weeks as a hitting partner and they split rooms. Keeps a mental runway number that he refreshes every Sunday and does not trust anyone else's arithmetic. Deeply suspicious of "AI apps", of subscriptions in general (cancelled everything last year), and of anything that wants his voice notes. Has a Buy Me a Coffee page that made US$340 total and he found the whole thing embarrassing. After a bad loss he goes for a run and tells nobody. Will push back on every task, ask where numbers come from, and try to break the prototype. Unconnected to Manu; came from a player WhatsApp group. Shown the Arya prototype.

## P6 · Nikola Vukovic · Stage 3 · parent as manager
25, Serbian, right-handed. ATP #240, career high #211. His father handles every booking, every entry and most of the money from Belgrade, and keeps everything in his head and on paper. A part-time coach travels to about half his events; a physio is booked ad hoc in the city he is in. The Serbian federation covers some travel through a grant. He plays Challenger 75 to 125 events across Europe and would like to be inside the top 200 by the end of the year, with 180 points to defend in the autumn. He does not know his own runway because his father does. Not on social media beyond a dormant Instagram. Would use a patron page only if his father agreed. After a bad loss he calls his father, who tells him what to do next; he does not write anything down. Introduced by a coach who knows Manu. Shown the Neumayer prototype.

## P7 · Aiko Tanaka · Stage 3 · WTA, full team, privacy-minded
28, Japanese, right-handed. WTA #190, career high #154. Travels with a full-time coach and a hitting partner; the Japan Tennis Association covers part of her travel and a Japanese apparel brand supplies kit and a modest retainer. Has a long relationship with one stringer in Tokyo and ships frames to him between swings; on the road she uses tournament stringers and always specifies tension in kilograms with a note about the balls. Her coach and her mother handle money and she prefers it that way. Speaks careful English, thinks in Japanese; would want to use the app in Japanese, which is not offered. Very private: keeps a paper diary after matches and would never dictate it to a phone, and dislikes the idea of any note being "read by agents." Has fans who send gifts and letters; a paid tier would feel wrong to her but her coach thinks she should consider it. Unconnected to Manu; came through a stringer's introduction. Shown the Neumayer prototype.

## P8 · Callum Reid · Stage 3 · late career, thinking ahead
29, British, from Glasgow, left-handed. ATP #310 in singles, #140 in doubles, which increasingly pays the bills. Married, one child, home in Barcelona. A UK racquet distributor pays him a small sponsorship and he would like a bigger one. Coach part-time; a doubles partner he travels with most weeks. Tracks money loosely in a banking app and knows roughly, not precisely, how long it lasts; his wife knows precisely. Has had two knee surgeries and is careful about what he says publicly about injuries. Thinking about the next three years and what a patron programme would look like as a bridge to coaching. Would trust a machine shortlist for singles less than for doubles, which the product does not model at all. After a bad loss he talks to his doubles partner on the drive to the hotel. Unconnected to Manu; came from a federation list. Shown the Neumayer prototype.
