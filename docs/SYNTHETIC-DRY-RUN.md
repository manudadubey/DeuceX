# Synthetic dry run of the research kit

Version 0.1, 13 September 2026, owner Manu Dubey, companion to RESEARCH-KIT.md.

> **Read this first.** Every participant here is an invented persona played by a language model. Nothing in this document is evidence about real players. The patterns reflect the documents the model was given and its own priors, and will tend to confirm what the pack already believes. The purpose is to test the kit, rehearse the analysis step and produce hypotheses for the real sessions.

## 2. Themes

**Promoted to findings (six or more of eight, or a clean split).**

*Wants to see the flight behind the estimate, from their own airport.* Eight of eight. P3 "I don't trust a machine's flight", P7 "my flights are from Tokyo, it is three times this". Four also wanted their own hotel numbers (P2, P4, P5, P6).

*The note is the score plus one fact; the real thing stays out.* Eight of eight. P8 "I'm not saying the knee into a phone". Also eight of eight: the mood picker was refused or left blank in every session (P3 "I'm 23, not 13"), and the Mindset patterns page was opened only by P7, who closed it.

*"Approve and publish" means it has gone.* Eight of eight predicted a send; none read the consequence line before tapping; five named the word "publish" as the reason (P2, P3, P4, P6, P8). P5 "what did I approve, the words, or the sending?"

*The asking is the problem, not the page.* Six of eight (P1, P2, P3, P5, P6, P7). P6 "it's the asking that's the problem, not the page", P7 "it feels like I am selling the letter". Seven of eight said A$29 was too high for the first tier; four named A$10 to A$15 instead.

*The fee is a manager's cut for no manager.* Six of eight used the manager or agent phrasing (P3 to P8); P1 called it a tax and P2 a door charge. Seven of eight would tolerate A$30.

*Australian dollars did not mean anything.* Six of eight looked for their own currency (P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P8); P7 priced in yen without asking.

*"This is not me."* Six of eight said so in their first sentence with the device (P1, P2, P4, P5, P6, P7). A stimulus finding, not a product finding; see section 5.

*The free tier is what I already have.* Seven of eight (all but P4) read the described Free view as a fair paywall and said the shortlist plus conditions brief was enough or already theirs (P5 "free's enough for me, which isn't what you want to hear").

**Clean split that is itself the finding.** Who holds the runway number. P2, P3 and P5 keep it themselves and answered the silence with a number; P1, P4, P6, P7 and P8 handed it to a mother, father, coach or wife. The split follows who manages the money, not stage, and predicts the three-answers score, the A$49 buyer and the sign-up intent almost exactly.

**Below the threshold, kept so nothing is lost.** Points over prize as the basis of the ranking (P2, P4, P6, P8; weighted to Stage 3). Looked for a send button to forward the brief or the deadline (P1, P4, P6, P7, P8). Disputed the direction or size of the tension step (P2, P3, P7, P8). Asked where the audio goes (P5, P7, P8). Wanted a two-person account or price (P6, P8). Would keep the receipts-to-runway loop above everything else (P1, P2, P3, P5, P8) against the conditions brief (P7), the patron page (P4) and the shortlist (P6); the same five would keep the trial only if the runway matched their own number. The memo card ("GPT-4o, validated with Zod") was ignored in five records and praised in none.

## 3. Findings against the documents

*D: A$60/point and PRD-01 (cost-to-prize ranking).* Not confirmed: zero of eight trusted it outright, four Partly, four No. The A$60 constant was tested once (P5), after the moderator explained it, and rejected ("a point at 487 isn't worth what a point at 710 is worth"). The cost side failed in all eight for the same reason: a Vienna route and a coach block that were not the player's. Change: a visible fare source, a home airport, a hospitality switch, and for Stage 3 a points-first ordering (P6, P8). Caveat: the persona sheet gave P3 and P5 spreadsheets and suspicion, and their answers reproduce that.

*A1 and A2 (patron fee, 8 or 10 percent, gross or net).* Suggests a change, weakly. Eight of eight resented A$48 and seven tolerated A$30, which argues for the Elite rate on both tiers, but six would not build the page at any fee, so the fee is second order to the asking. Gross versus net was raised once (P5) and is unresolved. P4 noticed the flat 10 percent in the payout table, which muddied her answer (the persona saw the pre-v0.3 table described in the materials; the live prototype now shows 8 percent).

*A7, M-GATE-1 and M-GATE-2 (approval gate and consequence line).* Contradicted as built. Seven of eight predicted a real send and none read the line first. The evidence points at the label, not the line: rename to "Approve draft" or "Queue" (P2, P8). The two-step confirm on Accept entry was tested once (P4) and worked. The dashboard one-tap question in A7 was not reached.

*A15 (Free tier scope).* M-TIER-1 confirmed, eight of eight read the dimmed page as locked, not broken. But seven of eight said the free shortlist plus conditions brief is enough for them. Suggests a decision: move the conditions brief behind Pro, or accept Free as the product for parent-managed and team players. Personas were told the tier table, so treat this lightly.

*Section D tension constants.* Boundary confirmed, seven of eight restated the test as a proposal. The constants are unresolved: the one-kilo step was disputed by four (three on direction for cold clay, one on size), 28°C was called about right once (P2) and too early for clay once (P4), altitude was news to two Stage 3 players (P6, P8), the frames ladder was contradicted once (P4). "You and your stringer decide" drew a correction from three who said the player decides and the stringer strings (P2, P3, P6).

*Section E (stringer share scope).* Suggests a change. Three would forward the brief to a stringer or coach as a screenshot (P1, P4, P7), two looked for a send button on it, five wanted some outbound share. A share of the brief looks cheaper and more wanted than a stringer login.

*M-STG-1 and M-STG-2 (stage detection and pin).* Contradicted for two populations: three WTA personas have no ATP number and would detect as Stage 1 (the moderator pinned P4 and P7 by hand), and three (P1, P2, P5) looked for UTR, which Stage 2 hides.

*PRD-00 dashboard, the three answers.* Partly confirmed: five Yes, two Partly, one No, the misses explained by who holds the money; first unprompted touch was the ranking chart in five of eight.

*A$49 and A$149 (PRD-00 section 4).* A$49: three comfortable, four partly, one too much; the buyer is the player in five cases and a parent in three (P1, P6, P7). A$149: eight of eight No, and nobody named an Elite feature they wanted (P4 "the forecast thing, I'd rather not know"). P8 unprompted reinvented the reverted Team pack ("if it did Dan and me together, then maybe the bigger one"). Caveat: the persona sheet pre-assigned every funder, and the sessions returned that assignment.

*Sharing scope (parent/manager link).* Confirmed once (P1 "runway, P&L, no notes, that's actually fine"), wanted twice (P6, P8).

## 5. What the dry run says about the kit itself

*Stimulus fit.* All eight records open with a mismatch note: stage (P1), sex and ranking body (P2, P4, P7), money band (P3), geography and currency (P5, P6), doubles (P8). The kit has no rule for a player the two prototypes do not fit, and the mismatch coloured every later answer ("pretending", "but this is not my list").

*Leading and assuming questions.* Task 1's "these three boxes" overrode the unprompted first touch (P6, P7). Task 2 assumes an enterable event and a solo entry with a coach block (P1, P2, P5, P8). Task 3 assumes voice capture and a performance in front of the founder in a second language (P4, P7). Task 6's success criterion fights the button label (P3, P4, P6). Discovery question 2 assumes the player paid (P6); question 4 read as intrusive (P3); the runway silence has no follow-up for a player who already has the number (P5).

*Pricing anchors.* "One restring" failed for a self-stringer (P2, "three restrings") and a Stage 3 player stringing four frames a week (P6). "Two hotel nights" was corrected by six of eight (cheaper hotels, hospitality, team books them). Australian dollars were converted in the head in six sessions. "Whose money" came fourth and was the real first question for the three parent-funded players.

*Timing.* All eight sessions ran over, by one to four minutes: P4 by one, P1, P2, P3, P5 and P8 by two, P6 and P7 by four. The overruns were spent well (held silences, a Settings detour that found the WTA gap), but pricing was squeezed to nine minutes in P5 and rushed in P8, and P2 had declared a hard stop at minute three. Seven tasks do not fit in 27 minutes for a player who reads every cost row.

*Where the moderator broke the kit's own rules.* Eleven breaks, at least one in every session: broke a silence after five seconds and got a courtesy answer (P1); said "it's just a draft" before the approve tap (P2); explained the accommodation figure and mentioned Fuel off-script (P3); explained the "why" paragraph (P4); explained the A$60 constant and said "it's only a demo" when asked where the audio goes (P5); explained runway before the tile was read (P6); explained the Neumayer sample and asked a player to tap after she had declined (P7); pointed at the ratio bar (P8). Every held silence produced the best line of its session.

*Wording that did not translate.* "Runway" (P1, P7 "money weeks"), "patrons" (P1, P7 "like Patreon?"), "Inside Track" (P7), "ratio" (P2), "A$" (P3), "Whisper" read as a person (P7).

*Recruiting gaps.* Three WTA players, all shown a men's build; the stage quota is blind to sex. One doubles-first player and no doubles model to test against. Three players whose real buyer is a parent, and the kit interviews only the player. P1 at 18 shows how close the Stage 1 cohort runs to minors, whom the analysis sections do not address. P8 disclosed an injury the record rightly treats as health information.

**Changes to make to RESEARCH-KIT.md before the real sessions.**

1. Add a stimulus rule: state which build each recruit sees, and either build Stage 1, WTA and USD-and-EUR variants or record those sessions as mismatch data.
2. Relabel the Content Agent button ("Approve draft") or test both labels, and rescore task 6 on the line rather than the label.
3. Rewrite task 1 to record the unprompted first touch before the "three boxes" sentence is read.
4. Give task 2 a stage-appropriate shortlist and a home airport, with hospitality handled for Challenger main draws.
5. Add a typed-note variant to task 3 and a private-recording option where the moderator steps out.
6. Ask "whose money" first in pricing for any player who said in discovery that a parent or partner holds the money.
7. Set the restring and hotel anchors from the player's own D5 and last-month answers, and read prices in the player's currency.
8. Branch discovery question 2 for players who did not pay ("who paid, and do they know the runway?") and add "would you trust the app's version over yours?" for players who already know their number.
9. The flat 10 percent in the prototype payout table is already fixed (v0.3, 8 percent on gross with Stripe separate); update the fee question's bracketed note in section 5 so the moderator does not read a stale caveat.
10. Add a "do not explain, even if asked" reminder for tasks 2, 3 and 6, with a scripted answer to "where does the audio go?" that does not close the question.
11. Decide whether unprompted exploration may eat pricing time, and either drop one task or extend the session to 70 minutes.
12. Add sex, doubles and "who is the buyer" to the recruiting quota, and a parental consent line for minors to section 2.
13. Add a data-handling line so an injury or mental-health remark is treated as health information in the record.

## 6. Hypotheses for the real sessions

1. Players will not accept the cost side of the shortlist unless the fare source and home airport are visible; a visible fare moves trust from No to Partly. Stage 2. Changes PRD-01 and D: A$60/point.
2. Stage 3 players order events by points and entry list, not prize, and reject a money-first ranking. Stage 3. Changes PRD-01 ordering.
3. "Publish" on the button causes a predicted send whatever the line says; a relabelled button passes task 6 without a stronger line. All stages. Changes A7, M-GATE-1.
4. Players whose money is held by a parent, coach or partner disown the runway tile; for them the manager link is the product. Parent-funded Stage 1, team-supported Stage 3. Changes the PRD-00 dashboard premise and the Sharing scope.
5. The felt floor for a first patron tier is A$10 to A$15; A$29 is called too much by most. All stages. Changes the TIERS defaults.
6. The 8 percent fee reads as a manager's cut and 5 percent as a bank fee, but neither changes whether a player builds the page; the asking does. All stages. Changes A1, A2.
7. No individual in the band pays A$149; Elite sells only to a parent, a brand manager or a two-player pack. All stages, doubles pairs especially. Changes PRD-00 section 4 and reopens the reverted Team pack.
8. A real match note is the score plus one fact, the mood picker is skipped and the Mindset patterns page is never opened unprompted. All stages. Changes the Match Scribe and Mindset Coach requirements.
9. Players dispute the step direction for cold clay and prefer a half-kilo step driven by the ball; altitude is new to some Stage 3 players. Stage 2 and 3 players who set their own tension. Changes the Section D constants.
10. A Free tier that includes the conditions brief is enough for parent-managed and team players, so the brief, not the cost columns, is the paid feature. Stage 1 and 3. Changes A15.

## 7. Sign-up intent and courtesy

Two asked outright: P3 ("can I try it with my own numbers?") and P4 ("can I sign up? is there a women's version?", for the patron page rather than the product). One asked indirectly: P1 ("can I see it with an ITF number?"), whose real buyer is his mother. Three were conditional: P2 (a women's version), P6 (a login for his father), P8 (doubles and a two-player price). P5 asked "is my data sold?" instead and P7 whether the recording would be deleted. The moderator followed the section 1 rule every time and made no promise.

Every record flagged at least one courtesy answer, eleven in all, each after a No said to the founder's face: P1 "maybe when I'm 500 in the world"; P2 "probably fine for someone more comfortable with it"; P3 "the app is clean, though"; P4 "the transcript editing is nice"; P5 "it's a nice brief though"; P6 "it's probably normal, you have costs"; P7 "maybe the free profile, in Japanese"; P8 "it's a good summary though". One (P1) was produced directly by the moderator breaking a silence. None was counted in the sheet, which is the kit working as written. They cluster on task 5 and the fee question, where the kit's fifth risk says they will.
